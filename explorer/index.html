        How the standards are delivered through IARs and data catalogues

        One asset, three records of it: the IAR answers who is accountable and how it is protected and retained; the data catalogue answers what it is and how to find and use it; the records system holds the evidence and controls disposal. The crosswalk maps the GKIM IAR guide's mandatory fields to DCAT 3 and records-management metadata, so one entry can feed both registers.

        AI helps deliver every cluster, and every cluster is what makes AI governable

        Two directions. AI for governance: where AI can do or speed up the work of each capability cluster. Governance for AI: what AI governance needs from data and information governance, and what each capability must take from AI governance in return.

        Two directions

        AI for governance

        Classify, tag and describe at scale; draft metadata and catalogue entries; flag quality issues; support appraisal and sensitivity review; find information for requests; remediate the digital heap.

        Each use is itself an AI system that needs an owner, an impact assessment, a transparency record where in scope, and records of what it decided.

        Governance for AI

        AI is only as safe as what it is given and what it leaves behind: known, owned, described, lawful and current inputs; recorded provenance; and prompts, outputs and actions kept as records where they evidence decisions.

        The accountability model's AI-readiness questions and the AI-ready datasets guidelines both start from the register and the catalogue.
