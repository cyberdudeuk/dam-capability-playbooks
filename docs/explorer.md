# Asset governance explorer

**Use it:** https://cyberdudeuk.github.io/dam-capability-playbooks/explorer/

A standalone page showing where data asset management and information asset management meet. It covers:

- the 32 DAM capabilities seen from the information world
- the 13 information-only capabilities the playbooks do not cover
- an information wheel for knowledge, information, records and enterprise content management
- standards and maturity models, and an IAR to DCAT crosswalk
- a cross-government landscape scan, AI in delivery and governance, skills, operating models, a governance calendar and three horizons

It has a wayfinder, eight illustrative personas with journeys, nine pathways including a guided tour, a user guide, an In short line on every view, and a Display menu (larger text, easier spacing, reduced motion, high contrast).

**Status:** prototype, version 2.3.0 (16 November 2026). Not government policy or guidance. Mappings, tags and personas are proposals for discussion and are labelled as such on the page.

## Who it is for

Data and information leads across the public sector: senior leaders, heads of team and leads in data governance, knowledge and information management, records management and AI delivery, and the information asset owners they support.

## How it is produced

The files in `explorer/` are the **public edition** of a working version used inside a team. The working version holds internal planning material: draft objectives, meeting-derived actions, a joint timeline and role actions. It is not in this repository.

The public edition is generated by a transform that removes that material. Every removal is an exact match that must be found the expected number of times, so a change to the working version fails the transform instead of leaking. The working version's own checks then run on the result, followed by a leak scan over the whole file.

Do not edit `explorer/` by hand. Raise an issue through the contact form and the owner will regenerate it.

The public edition:

- loads nothing from another site, with no web fonts
- carries a Content Security Policy that allows scripts only from this site
- keeps its markup, styles and script in separate files
- passes ESLint with no findings

## Checks

The page runs its own checks on every load and shows the result in the masthead. The public edition runs 22 checks:

- **Content:** K1 to K3 and K8 to K16 (capabilities, gaps, standards, the crosswalk, the landscape, AI rows, calendar, operating models and skills frameworks, all with sources)
- **Navigation:** K18 to K23 (further gaps, personas, journeys and pathways, the wayfinder, the lenses, the glossary)
- **Information wheel and summaries:** K25 and K26
- **Icons:** K27
- **Leak guard:** K24, public edition only

Checks K4 to K7 and K17 cover material that only the working version holds.

In this repository:

- `tests/test_explorer.py` guards against internal material, the Content Security Policy and remote loads. Each guard has a test showing it fails on a deliberate break.
- Browser check B-19 runs the page's self-checks, drills down the wheel by link and keyboard, and scans the rendered page. Its break, `--break explorer`, proves it fails.
- B-01 runs axe (WCAG 2.2 AA) on the page in light and dark mode. Every view and interactive state of both editions was also audited with axe on 16 November 2026, with no violations.

## The case against, and the answer

**The case against.** Knowledge and information management already has its own frameworks, registers and assessment tools. A second capability model risks a parallel stack, adds burden for the organisations asked to use it, and blurs who owns what. Organisations that run separate data, information and AI registers rarely reconcile them.

**The answer.** The explorer proposes no new framework. It reuses the DAM playbooks' structure, shows where existing information management practice already covers ground, and limits the gap list to capabilities neither covers. The information wheel is a way of seeing the information world alongside the DAMA wheel, not a replacement for any standard. Every join-up it suggests is framed as one ask, one register entry or one owner, not two.

**Limits.**

- No published figure was found on how often joint data and information governance succeeds in government, so none is given.
- The landscape scan is a sample of developments found through web searches on 16 November 2026, not a census.
- The tags (relationship, lens, transformation, horizon) and the personas are the author's judgement.
