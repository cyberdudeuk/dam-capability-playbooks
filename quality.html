    Data quality

    Is the data good enough for what it is used for, and are we fixing what is not?

    • 8 steps
    • 8 roles
    • 3 templates
    • 6 learning modules

    In brief

    • Every critical data asset needs a data quality action plan (DQAP) or an equivalent.
    • Agree what 'good enough' means with users, test it with rules, and fix the causes of failures.
    • Publish how good the data is, including what it should not be used for.
    Easy read summary
    • Data quality means data is right and fit for use.
    • This page shows how to check data and make it better.
    • It has rules you can download and run.

    Why it matters

    Know, prove and improve the quality of each critical data asset through a data quality action plan (DQAP), scored with the government issues framework, reported openly and repeated every cycle.

    Every identified asset must have a DQAP or equivalent (DAM-R06), and existing processes should cover all seven DQAP steps (DAM-R07).

    Before you start

    • A designated asset with a named data owner
    • Agreement from at least two user teams on what the data is used for
    • The DQAP template from GOV.UK, or the Data Quality Commons tools

    What you will have at the end

    • A rule catalogue with targets
    • A baseline and issue log
    • A prioritised action plan
    • A plain-English data quality statement

    • Cataloguing 4: Confirm ownership Quality 1: Identify critical data
      The owner on the catalogue record is the owner who signs off DQAP targets and accepts risks. (IL-02)
    • Cataloguing 3: Test and designate Quality 1: Identify critical data
      The asset boundary set at designation is the scope of the DQAP. No DQAP for fields outside the asset. (IL-03)
    • Quality 6: Report openly Cataloguing 5: Describe
      Quality information is published on the catalogue record (IL-04)
    • Quality 6: Report openly Governance 4: Operate
      Quality issues above Medium enter the board's issue and risk log. (IL-05)
    • Cataloguing 8: Maintain and review Quality 8: Review every year
      One annual review date per asset covers the record (IL-07)

    Your progress through data quality0 of 8 done

    1. 1

      Step 1: Identify critical data

      Not started

      Know which data items in each asset matter most.

      When: The asset is designated and has an owner.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 1

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Name the 5 to 15 data items that drive the most important decisions (critical data elements).
      2. Record who uses the data, for what, and who or what it describes.
      3. Log known issues in the DQAP issues log.

      You will have

      • Criticality matrix
      • User and purpose map

      Check before you move on

      • Critical data elements agreed with at least two user teams
      • You know which groups the data might miss

      Meets: DAM-R06

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Quality assurance of administrative dataS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it; Government Data Quality HubV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    2. 2

      Step 2: Define rules and targets

      Not started

      Turn 'good enough' into rules a computer can test.

      When: Critical data elements are agreed.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 2

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Choose the dimensions that matter - completeness, uniqueness, consistency, timeliness, validity, accuracy.
      2. Write each rule in plain English first, then as a testable rule.
      3. Set targets from the issues framework range for importance times impact.
      4. Store rules as files (a data contract) so they survive a change of tool or supplier.

      You will have

      • Rule catalogue
      • Targets
      • Data contract

      Check before you move on

      • Every critical data element has at least one rule
      • Targets come from user impact, not from what the data already achieves

      Meets: DAM-R06 DAM-R15

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Data on the Web Best Practices: Data Quality VocabularyV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Code of Practice for StatisticsV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Open Data Contract Standard (ODCS)V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    3. 3

      Step 3: Measure a baseline

      Not started

      Know where you are today.

      When: Rules and targets are agreed.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 3

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Profile the data and run every rule; record the date and data version.
      2. Log every failure as an issue with its size and who it affects.
      3. Break results down by group where you lawfully can.

      You will have

      • Baseline results
      • Issue log

      Check before you move on

      • Results are reproducible
      • Group differences looked for and recorded

      Meets: DAM-R06

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Quality assurance of administrative dataS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it; The AQuA BookV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    4. 4

      Step 4: Prioritise and set goals

      Not started

      Fix what hurts most first.

      When: The baseline is recorded.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 4

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Score each issue with the issues framework.
      2. Resolve Critical immediately, High within one cycle, Medium within three and Low within six.
      3. Agree the order with the owner and record accepted risks with a review date.

      You will have

      • Prioritised issue list
      • Goals in the DQAP
      • Risk acceptances

      Check before you move on

      • Every issue has a priority and a timescale
      • Issues affecting some groups more than others are not ranked down as 'small'

      Meets: DAM-R15

      Frameworks and standards for this step: The Green Book and accompanying guidanceV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; The Orange Book: management of riskV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    5. 5

      Step 5: Fix causes at source

      Not started

      Stop problems coming back.

      When: Goals are agreed.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 5

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Ask why until you reach the cause - people, process, system, supplier or standard.
      2. Prefer prevention at entry over downstream cleaning.
      3. Put supplier duties and measures into contracts.
      4. Re-run the rule to prove the fix worked.

      You will have

      • Root cause records
      • Actions with owners
      • Evidence of fixes

      Check before you move on

      • Every fix proved by re-running the rule
      • Any downstream cleaning is recorded and temporary

      Meets: DAM-R06

      Frameworks and standards for this step: CSV on the Web (CSVW) primerV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Identifying property and street information (UPRN and USRN)V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Prevent technical debt and legacyV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    6. 6

      Step 6: Report openly

      Not started

      Users know how good the data is and what it should not be used for.

      When: Each measurement cycle.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 6

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Update a plain-English data quality statement - scores, trends, known issues and unsuitable uses.
      2. Publish quality information with the catalogue record (W3C DQV) so it travels with the asset.
      3. Report the status of all assets to the governance board; quality issues enter the issue and risk log.

      You will have

      • Data quality statement
      • Quality metadata on the catalogue record
      • Board report

      Check before you move on

      • Statement says what the data is not fit for
      • Quality information sits on the catalogue record

      Meets: DAM-R06

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Code of Practice for StatisticsV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Data on the Web Best Practices: Data Quality VocabularyV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    7. 7

      Step 7: Repeat and sustain

      Not started

      Quality stays good without heroics.

      When: After the first cycle.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Step 7

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Run checks automatically on each load or on a fixed cycle.
      2. Track trends and alert when a rule drops below target.
      3. Escalate with the agreed thresholds.
      4. Keep rules, results and the plan in version control.

      You will have

      • Automated checks
      • Trend record
      • Escalation log

      Check before you move on

      • Checks run without manual effort
      • The steward has a named deputy

      Meets: DAM-R06 DAM-R07

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Government Data Quality HubV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Open Data Contract Standard (ODCS)V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    8. 8

      Step 8: Review every year

      Not started

      Rules and targets still match what users need.

      When: Every 12 months, on the same date as the catalogue record review.Accountable: Data ownerDQAP Added by the Commons

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Review rules, targets and the plan with the owner and users.
      2. Add rules for new uses, including AI.
      3. Retire rules that no longer matter; raise targets that are now easy.

      You will have

      • Annual review note
      • Next year's goals

      Check before you move on

      • Reviewed with the catalogue record, not separately
      • New uses assessed against the AI-ready data guidance

      Meets: DAM-R04 DAM-R07

      Frameworks and standards for this step: The Magenta BookV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Government data maturity hubV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    Principles and dimensions

    From The Government Data Quality FrameworkV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05.

    Five principles

    1. Commit to data quality
    2. Know your users and their needs
    3. Assess quality throughout the data lifecycle
    4. Communicate data quality clearly and effectively
    5. Anticipate changes affecting data quality

    Six dimensions

    • Completeness
      How fully records are present
    • Uniqueness
      Whether each thing is recorded once
    • Consistency
      Whether values agree with other values about the same thing
    • Timeliness
      Whether data is up to date for its use
    • Validity
      Whether data is in the expected range and format
    • Accuracy
      How closely data matches reality

    Roles

    • Data owner (OWN)V: Verified against the primary source
      Senior leader accountable for the meaning, content, quality and management of the asset; approves changes and signs off targets and risks.
    • Data steward (STW)V: Verified against the primary source
      Runs the asset day to day: policies and standards, access, quality processes and documentation such as the business glossary in the catalogue.
    • Data custodian (CUS)V: Verified against the primary source
      Technical expert for the system holding the data: storage, access controls, auditable change and disposal.
    • Data quality lead or analyst (DQL)V: Verified against the primary source
      Methods, profiling, tooling and reporting for data quality.
    • Data users (USR)V: Verified against the primary source
      Teams, services and other organisations that rely on the data; say what they need and report problems.
    • Data governance board (BRD)P: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published
      Sets policy direction, resolves escalations and sees every asset's status. Chaired by the CDO, SIRO or equivalent.
    • Catalogue manager (CAT)O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance
      Runs the organisation's catalogue, its metadata standard and its harvest feed.
    • People the data describes (PUB)P: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published
      Citizens, communities and their representatives; help spot exclusion and test whether quality information is understandable.
