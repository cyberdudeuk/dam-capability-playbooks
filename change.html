    Delivery and change

    How to make the DAM Policy stick: in the yearly business-as-usual cycle, and inside the programmes and projects that create, change and retire data assets.

    • 16 practices
    • 56 actions
    • 6 life cycle stages

    In brief

    • Most critical data assets are created or replaced by change programmes, so controls belong in them.
    • Each item lists actions, evidence, roles and the rules behind it.
    • Tick actions as you go; progress stays in your browser.

    DAM across the delivery life cycle

    Stages follow the GOV.UK Service Manual phases and the project delivery standard. Use it as an overlay on your own programme plan.

    1. Strategy and business case

      • List the critical data assets the change will create, change, depend on or retire.
      • Cost ownership, cataloguing and quality work in the economic and financial cases.
      • Add data asset risks to the programme risk register.

      CH-01 CH-03 CH-05 CH-09 CH-12

    2. Discovery

      • Check the catalogue for assets that already meet the need before collecting new data.
      • Agree who will own each new or changed asset.

      CH-06 CH-13 CH-16

    3. Alpha

      • Design metadata and quality rules alongside the data model.
      • Run a data protection impact assessment where personal data is involved.

      CH-02 CH-14

    4. Beta and build

      • Catalogue new assets before go-live, with the core metadata.
      • Write and test the DQAP; reconcile migrated data against the source.

    5. Live and business as usual

      • Hand ownership, the DQAP and the runbook to named people in business as usual.
      • Report the asset in the next annual return.

      CH-07 CH-08 CH-10 CH-11 CH-15

    6. Migrate or retire

      • Start from the asset register; decide to migrate, archive or dispose of each asset.
      • Update or close catalogue entries and transfer ownership formally.

      CH-04

    • CH-01 · Change programmes · Strategy and business case

      Put data assets in the business case

      Not started

      The policy says data must be valued and invested in; business cases are where that money is decided.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Name each critical data asset in scope of the change in the strategic case.
      2. Cost owner, steward and catalogue effort and DQAP remediation in the financial case.
      3. Count avoided duplicate collection and cleansing as benefits in the economic case.
      4. Name the data owner in the management case.

      Evidence

      • Asset list in the business case
      • Costed DAM activities
      • Named owners

      Roles

      Chief data officer Data owner Lead policy coordinator Senior responsible owner

    • CH-02 · Change programmes · Alpha

      Use spend control and service assessments as checkpoints

      Not started

      Spend control and service assessments already stop work that is not ready; adding DAM evidence costs little.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 3 done

      Actions

      1. Add three questions to internal spend control: which assets, who owns them, are they catalogued.
      2. At alpha, show the planned metadata and quality rules; at beta, the catalogue entry and DQAP.
      3. Record the answers so the next return reuses them.

      Evidence

      • Spend control submission notes
      • Assessment evidence pack

    • CH-04 · Change programmes · Migrate or retire

      Plan legacy exits from the asset register

      Not started

      Legacy systems hold large concentrations of hard-to-reach data; the legacy guidance asks for an asset register first.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Build or update the register of data assets held in the legacy system.
      2. Profile quality before migration and set reconciliation rules for counts, totals and keys.
      3. Decide for each asset: migrate, archive under records rules, or dispose.
      4. Keep the catalogue and ownership in step as each asset moves.

      Evidence

      • Asset register for the system
      • Profiling and reconciliation results
      • Disposition decisions

    • CH-05 · Business as usual and change · Strategy and business case

      Target legacy where it blocks outcomes and widens inequalities

      Not started

      Where poor or locked-in data delays access to services, the people most affected are often those already disadvantaged.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Score each critical asset on legacy risk, quality, reuse value and impact on outcomes.
      2. Check whether gaps or errors fall harder on some groups, using the equality duty.
      3. Fund fixes first where risk, value and inequality overlap.
      4. Report the choice and its reasons to the data governance board.

      Evidence

      • Prioritised asset heatmap
      • Equality analysis

    • CH-09 · Business as usual and change · Strategy and business case

      Plan people change, not just process change

      Not started

      New duties fail when people are not told, trained, given time or recognised for them.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Assess who is affected: owners, stewards, custodians, analysts, suppliers.
      2. Put data duties into role descriptions and objectives.
      3. Train each role at the right level and give stewards protected time.
      4. Communicate why the policy matters in terms of services and people, not compliance.

      Evidence

      • Change impact assessment
      • Training plan
      • Communications plan

    • CH-14 · Change programmes · Alpha

      Design out future legacy and lock-in

      Not started

      Systems bought or built today become tomorrow's legacy unless their data can be moved out without loss or delay.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Require data in open, documented formats with a published schema.
      2. Ask suppliers to show how a full export with metadata works before you sign.
      3. Prefer open standards and APIs that follow the government API standards.
      4. Record each system's exit route against its assets in the catalogue.

      Evidence

      • Exit and portability test results
      • Schema and API documentation

    • CH-15 · Business as usual · Live and business as usual

      Prepare for a cyber incident affecting a critical asset

      Not started

      A ransomware attack or breach can corrupt or expose a critical asset; quality plans and owners must be ready.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Actions done0 of 4 done

      Actions

      1. Mark critical data assets in the incident response plan and asset inventory.
      2. Agree recovery point and recovery time with each data owner.
      3. Test restoring an asset and check its quality afterwards against the DQAP rules.
      4. Agree who tells data users when an asset may be affected.

      Evidence

      • Incident plan entries
      • Restore test and post-restore quality results
