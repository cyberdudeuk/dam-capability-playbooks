    Tools and interoperability

    How catalogue, data quality, lineage, master data and governance tools can work together, which open standards connect them, and how to buy them without lock-in.

    • 9 kinds of tool
    • 19 open standards
    • 23 RFI questions

    In brief

    • 9 kinds of tool, mapped to DAMA knowledge areas, with the open standards that connect them.
    • 9 common pairings rated for how mature their open routes are.
    • 23 questions for a request for information or tender, as CSV.

    Most organisations run several tools for data asset management, often from different suppliers, and many are offered a bundled suite. This page sets out the kinds of tool involved, the open standards and patterns that let them work together, where bundles and contracts create lock-in, and questions to ask in a request for information (RFI) or tender, framed by what the DAM Policy needs. It names no products and endorses none; for named products and a fuller question builder, see the related links at the end of this page.

    Five principles

    1. Your metadata, glossary, quality rules, lineage and results are your data. Make sure you can get all of them out, in open formats, at any time and at no extra charge.
    2. Prefer open standards at the boundaries between tools, even when the tools come from one supplier. That keeps the option to replace one part without replacing the rest.
    3. Buy capabilities, not bundles. Price, evaluate and be able to exit each capability separately.
    4. Prove interoperability before you sign. A round-trip test with your own data is worth more than a list of connectors.
    5. Plan the exit at the start: cost it, time it and write it into the contract and business case.

    Kinds of tool

    Integration patterns

    How tools connect, and the lock-in risk each pattern carriesO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Integration patterns
    PatternHow it worksUse it forLock-in riskWatch out for
    Scan and harvestThe catalogue or lineage tool connects to a database, warehouse, file store or business intelligence tool and reads its technical metadata on a schedule.Building and refreshing the inventory of technical assets.MediumConnectors are often licensed separately, priced per source, or maintained only for the supplier's own platform. Ask who maintains each one and how quickly it follows new versions.
    Documented APIOne tool reads or writes another's records through a published API, described in OpenAPI, with the same access controls as the user interface.Showing quality scores in the catalogue, creating issues, syncing owners or terms.LowCheck rate limits, whether every object is available through the API (not only some), and whether API calls are charged.
    Events and webhooksA tool publishes an event when something changes, such as a rule failing, a pipeline running or an asset being classified, and others subscribe.Near real-time alerts, lineage capture and keeping several tools in step.LowPrefer open event formats such as OpenLineage over supplier-specific payloads.
    Open file exchangeExport and import in open formats such as DCAT (JSON-LD or Turtle), SKOS, CSV with CSVW, or ODCS YAML.Migration, exit, bulk loading, publishing and sharing with other organisations.LowExports that drop custom fields, history or links are a form of lock-in. Test a full round trip.
    Contract-driven checksProducers publish a data contract; quality and pipeline tools read the same file to check data, and the catalogue shows it.Agreeing what consumers can rely on, and checking it automatically.LowContract formats are still maturing; keep contracts in your own repository, not only inside a tool.
    Embedded viewsOne tool shows another's information in place, for example quality results on a catalogue page.Giving users one place to look without copying data.MediumEmbedding through a supplier's private integration can hide a dependency that breaks when either tool changes.
    Shared identity and accessAll tools sign users in through your identity provider and take roles and groups from it.Always. It keeps ownership and stewardship roles consistent across tools.LowSome products charge more for single sign-on or automated user provisioning; ask early.
    AI access through an open protocolAn assistant reads the catalogue, glossary and quality results through a documented API or a Model Context Protocol server.Plain-language questions over metadata, with answers that cite their source.MediumMake sure the assistant respects access rules and that prompts and answers stay in your approved environment.

    One supplier or several

    A bundled suite from one supplier and a set of tools from several suppliers can both work. What matters is whether you can change one part without changing the restO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    What a bundle can offer

    • One contract, one support route and one user interface.
    • Integrations between modules usually work on day one.
    • Can be quicker to start for a small team.

    Where a bundle can lock you in

    • Modules share a private metadata model, so replacing one part means replacing all of it.
    • Weaker modules are carried by stronger ones, and evaluated less carefully.
    • Pricing across modules is hard to compare with separate tools.
    • Connectors to other suppliers' tools are fewer, slower or charged extra.
    • The exit cost grows with every module adopted.

    Three tests for any bundle

    • Could you replace the data quality module next year and keep the catalogue? What would it cost?
    • Are the module prices shown separately, and can you drop a module at renewal without penalty?
    • Do the modules use the same open standards between themselves that they offer to other tools?

    Commercial barriers and how to avoid them

    Barriers that arise with catalogue, data quality and bundled toolsO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance. The Competition and Markets Authority's Cloud services market investigationV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05 found that data egress fees and barriers to interoperability restrict switching and multi-cloud, and raised concerns about software licensing in cloud; see the CMA announces package of actions on business software and cloud servicesV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05.

    Commercial barriers
    BarrierWhat happensHow to avoid it
    Pricing metric that punishes growthCharges per asset, per scanned table, per rule run or per user can make cataloguing everything, or running quality checks often, unaffordable. The DAM Policy expects broad coverage.Model cost at three and five times today's volume; prefer prices tied to things you control; cap annual increases.
    Shared credits or consumption poolsCredits spread across modules make it hard to see what each capability costs and easy to overspend.Ask for a price per capability and a monthly usage report you can export.
    Connectors and APIs charged extraSome connectors, API access, single sign-on or automated user provisioning sit in higher tiers.List every integration you need in the requirement and require them in the evaluated price.
    Export and exit chargesFees or effort to get data and metadata out, including cloud egress charges, make exit costly. The CMA found egress fees and interoperability barriers restrict switching in public cloud.Require complete export in open formats at any time at no extra charge, and an exit plan with supplier support in the contract.
    Ownership of rules, terms and lineageContracts can leave quality rules, glossary content, classifications or derived lineage as the supplier's property or in a form only their tool can read.State that all content you or the tool create about your data is yours, and must be exportable.
    Platform and marketplace commitmentsBuying through a cloud marketplace against a spend commitment can tie a catalogue or quality tool to one cloud platform.Check the tool runs and is supported on other platforms, and that the commitment does not lengthen the term.
    Licence terms that limit where software runsLicensing that costs more on a rival cloud reduces choice. The CMA has raised concerns about business software licensing in cloud.Ask for licence terms that are the same wherever the software runs.
    Long terms, auto-renewal and price upliftMulti-year terms, automatic renewal and uncapped increases reduce your bargaining power at exactly the point you might switch.Use break points, notice periods you can meet, and capped increases; diarise renewal decisions a year ahead.

    For services supplied into the European Union, the EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854)S: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it phases out switching charges, including egress charges for a switch. It is not UK law, but suppliers may offer the same terms to UK buyers: ask.

    Six steps to buy without lock-in

    1. Map what you have. List the tools in use or planned for each capability, who owns them, contract end dates and how they connect today.
    2. Set the boundaries. Decide which open standard sits at each boundary between capabilities, using the pairs on this page, and record it as an architecture decision.
    3. Run an RFI. Use the questions on this page. Search Find a Tender and G-Cloud for how other bodies specified similar tools.
    4. Test before you buy. In the proof of concept, run a round trip of export and import, show quality results in the catalogue through an open route, and capture lineage from a pipeline you own.
    5. Write it into the contract. Include export in open formats, content ownership, separate capability prices, capped increases and a costed exit plan.
    6. Keep checking. Repeat the export test each year before renewal, and record the result in your assurance evidence.

    These steps support Technology Code of Practice point 11: define your purchasing strategyV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05 and Technology Code of Practice point 4: make use of open standardsV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05, and the delivery and change practices on building DAM into contracts, designing out lock-in and buying routes and contract terms.

    Questions for a request for information or tender

    23 questions, grouped. Use them as written or adapt themO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance. Download them as CSV to load into your own RFI template. They focus on what the DAM Policy needs from a tool; for product-level questions, a lock-in checker and a test script, see Related elsewhere at the end of this page.

    Standards and export

    1. RFI-01 Which open standards does the product read and write for catalogue records, glossaries, quality results, lineage and data contracts? Give versions.
    2. RFI-02 Can we export everything, including custom fields, history, owners, classifications, comments and links, at any time, in open formats, without supplier help or extra charge?
    3. RFI-03 Describe a round trip, exporting our catalogue as DCAT and re-importing it into another product. What is lost?
    4. RFI-04 Does the product support the government Metadata Exchange Model for critical data assets?

    APIs, connectors and plugins

    1. RFI-05 Is every object and action in the user interface available through a documented API? Provide the OpenAPI description.
    2. RFI-06 List each connector, who builds and maintains it, its licence and price, and how quickly it follows new versions of the connected system.
    3. RFI-07 Which connectors exist for other suppliers' catalogues, quality tools, lineage tools and records systems?
    4. RFI-08 Does the product emit and accept OpenLineage events? Can it read ODCS data contracts?
    5. RFI-09 What rate limits, call charges or tier limits apply to APIs and events?

    Content ownership and portability

    1. RFI-10 Confirm that metadata, glossary terms, quality rules, scores, lineage and any content the product derives from our data belong to us.
    2. RFI-11 In what form are quality rules held? Can they be read and run outside the product?
    3. RFI-12 Can we keep data contracts and rules in our own code repository and have the product read them from there?

    Commercial terms and exit

    1. RFI-13 Give separate prices for each capability, and confirm we can stop any one at renewal without penalty.
    2. RFI-14 Model the price at today's volumes and at three and five times them.
    3. RFI-15 Which features, connectors, single sign-on or provisioning sit in higher tiers?
    4. RFI-16 What does exit involve, how long does it take, what support is included, and what does it cost, including any cloud egress?
    5. RFI-17 Are licence prices the same on any cloud or on our own infrastructure?

    Security, identity and AI

    1. RFI-18 Does the product use our identity provider for sign-in and automated user provisioning, at no extra charge?
    2. RFI-19 Can classification and access policies be read by other tools?
    3. RFI-20 If the product includes AI features, where are prompts and answers processed and stored, are they used to train models, and can they be switched off?
    4. RFI-21 Can an assistant we already approve query the product through an open protocol, respecting the same access rules?

    Evidence

    1. RFI-22 Name customers who use the product with another supplier's catalogue, quality or lineage tool, and how they connect them.
    2. RFI-23 Will you take part in a short proof of concept using our data and an interoperability test we define?
