    Data cataloguing

    What critical data do we hold, is it described well enough to find and reuse, and have we told GDS?

    • 8 steps
    • 8 roles
    • 4 templates
    • 7 learning modules

    In brief

    • Find the data other organisations depend on, and decide which of it is critical.
    • Describe each critical data asset well enough for someone outside your team to find and judge it.
    • Keep the descriptions in a catalogue, report them to GDS, and review them every year.
    Easy read summary
    • A data catalogue is a list of your data and what it is about.
    • This page shows how to build one and what to record.
    • It shows what you must send to the Government Digital Service.

    Why it matters

    Find the critical data assets, describe each one to the cross-government metadata standard, keep the descriptions in a catalogue that others can harvest, and report them to GDS every year.

    Identifying, cataloguing, reporting and annually reviewing metadata are all 'must' requirements (DAM-R01 to DAM-R04).

    Before you start

    • A named coordinating team, led by your chief data officer
    • Access to your data catalogue, information asset register and register of data sharing agreements
    • Ownership confirmed, or under way, through the governance Assign recipe

    What you will have at the end

    • A designation log with reasons
    • A catalogue record per asset
    • An assured return to GDS
    • An annual review record

    • Cataloguing 4: Confirm ownership Quality 1: Identify critical data
      The owner on the catalogue record is the owner who signs off DQAP targets and accepts risks. (IL-02)
    • Cataloguing 3: Test and designate Quality 1: Identify critical data
      The asset boundary set at designation is the scope of the DQAP. No DQAP for fields outside the asset. (IL-03)
    • Quality 6: Report openly Cataloguing 5: Describe
      Quality information is published on the catalogue record (IL-04)
    • Governance 2: Frame Cataloguing 5: Describe
      The metadata standard and the classification policy adopted in Frame are what records are checked against. (IL-06)

      One annual review date per asset covers the record (IL-07)

      The GDS return is part of the governance evidence pack (IL-08)

    Your progress through data cataloguing0 of 8 done

    1. 1

      Step 1: Mobilise

      Not started

      Have a named lead and a coordinating team before identification starts.

      When: Policy adoption begins, or a new reporting cycle opens.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. The CDO (or most senior data official) leads and appoints a coordinating team or person.
      2. Nominate the lead policy coordinator for the Working Group.
      3. Agree how the department will coordinate and assure its ALBs' returns.
      4. Bring in data owners, IAOs, stewards, service owners, analysts and policy makers.

      You will have

      • Named lead and coordinator
      • Contributor list
      • ALB coordination plan

      Check before you move on

      • Coordinator nominated to the Working Group
      • Every ALB has a named contact role

      Meets: DAM-R14 DAM-R03

      Frameworks and standards for this step: National Data LibraryV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Data Standards AuthorityV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Metadata standards for sharing and publishing dataV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    2. 2

      Step 2: Discover candidates

      Not started

      Build a long list of data other organisations depend on.

      When: The team is mobilised.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Start from what you already have - data catalogues, information asset registers and registers of data sharing agreements.
      2. Find data you provide to, or acquire from, other organisations for an essential process or purpose.
      3. Prioritise assets supporting government missions and your services (including Top 75 services).
      4. Include high-reuse-value assets not yet shared.

      You will have

      • Candidate list with source of each entry

      Check before you move on

      • All three starting registers searched
      • Both directions covered - data provided and data acquired

      Meets: DAM-R01

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Manage information: guidance for governmentV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Using metadata to describe data assets in a data catalogueS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it

    3. 3

      Step 3: Test and designate

      Not started

      Decide which candidates are critical data assets, and draw each asset's boundary.

      When: The candidate list is complete.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Apply the policy's scope tests - classified OFFICIAL, meets the critical data asset criteria, within your ownership accountabilities.
      2. Record which of the seven purposes each asset supports.
      3. Draw the boundary - only the fields needed for the cross-government purpose form the asset, not the whole system.
      4. If you designate a distribution, designate its source data set too.
      5. Record why each candidate was or was not designated.

      You will have

      • Designation decisions with reasons
      • Asset boundary per asset

      Check before you move on

      • Every asset passes all scope tests
      • Every asset has at least one purpose
      • Source data sets designated where a distribution is

      Meets: DAM-R01 DAM-R11 DAM-R12

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Government Security ClassificationsV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Data Protection Act 2018V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    4. 4

      Step 4: Confirm ownership

      Not started

      Every designated asset has a named, accountable owner before it is described.

      When: Assets are designated.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Confirm the owner (data owner or IAO) and steward for each asset with the governance Assign recipe.
      2. Use a shared team contact point for the asset, never a personal inbox.
      3. Record a successor so the metadata survives the owner leaving.

      You will have

      • Owner
      • steward and contact point per asset

      Check before you move on

      • No asset goes forward without a named owner
      • Contact point is a team route or form

      Meets: DAM-R05 DAM-R10

      Frameworks and standards for this step: What each role does in a service teamV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Government Knowledge and Information Management ProfessionV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    5. 5

      Step 5: Describe

      Not started

      Describe each asset well enough for someone outside your team to find, understand and judge it.

      When: The asset has an owner.Accountable: Data owner

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Record the properties in the metadata guidance (see the metadata tab).
      2. Write in plain English; expand acronyms; describe the data, and put usage warnings in usage notes.
      3. Use ISO 8601 dates, IANA media types, and 'unknown' or 'not applicable' rather than blanks.
      4. Model datasets, data services and distributions as the MEM and DCAT do.
      5. Add quality information from the DQAP (see data quality, stage 6).

      You will have

      • Catalogue record per asset
      • conforming to the metadata guidance

      Check before you move on

      • Title and description are plain English
      • Access rights, licence and security classification recorded
      • Dates in ISO 8601

      Meets: DAM-R02 DAM-R13

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Data Catalog Vocabulary (DCAT) version 3V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Using metadata to describe data assets in a data catalogueS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it; Metadata standards for sharing and publishing dataV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; SKOS: Simple Knowledge Organization System referenceV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; UK GEMINI metadata standardV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; ISO 23081-1:2017 Metadata for records: principlesS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it

    6. 6

      Step 6: Catalogue and expose

      Not started

      Keep the record in your catalogue in a form others can harvest.

      When: Records are complete.Accountable: Data owner

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Store each record in the organisation's catalogue, or an asset register where no catalogue exists yet.
      2. Expose records in DCAT so portals and the Discoverability Platform can harvest them.
      3. Make sure every link in a record works from outside your organisation.
      4. Choose catalogue tools against what any catalogue must expose, not a single vendor.

      You will have

      • Catalogue entries
      • Machine-readable feed

      Check before you move on

      • Records are reachable by harvest, not only by people
      • No record depends on an internal-only link

      Meets: DAM-R02 DAM-R16

      Frameworks and standards for this step: National Data LibraryV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Open Government Licence v3.0V: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; API technical and data standardsV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; OpenAPI SpecificationV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Making government datasets ready for AIV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    7. 7

      Step 7: Report to GDS

      Not started

      Submit an assured return on time.

      When: A return window opens (baseline January 2027).Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Prepare the return from catalogue records, not by retyping them.
      2. Collate and assure ALB returns.
      3. Check the return before submission so moderation is about judgement, not formatting.
      4. Submit through the interim template, or the Discoverability Platform when it is live.

      You will have

      • Assured return
      • ALB assurance record

      Check before you move on

      • Every asset in the return has an owner, a purpose and current metadata
      • ALB returns assured by the department

      Meets: DAM-R03

      Frameworks and standards for this step: Using metadata to describe data assets in a data catalogueS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it; Data Standards Authority: Standards CatalogueV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05

    8. 8

      Step 8: Maintain and review

      Not started

      Records stay true.

      When: Every 12 months, and whenever an asset or its owner changes.Accountable: Data owner

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Review every record at least every 12 months.
      2. Update records when the asset changes, and when the owner leaves.
      3. Retire assets that are no longer critical, with a reason.
      4. Feed findings into the governance Assure recipe.

      You will have

      • Annual review record
      • Updated catalogue entries

      Check before you move on

      • Every record reviewed in the last 12 months
      • Owner changes reflected within the month

      Meets: DAM-R04 DAM-R10

      Frameworks and standards for this step: PROV-O: the PROV ontologyV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; OpenLineageV: Verified against the primary source on 2026-11-05; Advice on retentionS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it; ISO 15489-1:2016 Records management: concepts and principlesS: Found by web search; the site blocked automated checking, so open it before relying on it

    Roles

    • Chief data officer (CDO)V: Verified against the primary source
      Implements the policy in the organisation and leads the identification exercise; sponsors governance.
    • Lead policy coordinator (COO)P: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published
      Coordinates identification, collates and assures the return (including ALBs), and is the link to the Working Group.
    • Data owner (OWN)V: Verified against the primary source
      Senior leader accountable for the meaning, content, quality and management of the asset; approves changes and signs off targets and risks.
    • Information asset owner (IAO)V: Verified against the primary source
      Accountable to the SIRO for protecting the whole information asset; may hold data owner duties under the IAO or hybrid option.
    • Data steward (STW)V: Verified against the primary source
      Runs the asset day to day: policies and standards, access, quality processes and documentation such as the business glossary in the catalogue.
    • Data custodian (CUS)V: Verified against the primary source
      Technical expert for the system holding the data: storage, access controls, auditable change and disposal.
    • Catalogue manager (CAT)O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance
      Runs the organisation's catalogue, its metadata standard and its harvest feed.
    • Data users (USR)V: Verified against the primary source
      Teams, services and other organisations that rely on the data; say what they need and report problems.

    Entity and relationship list (T-CAT-4)

    Business as usual and change

    A plain list of the entities in a data asset and how they relate, so a relationship diagram can be drawn from it. The Data architecture and models supplement has a worked example and the diagram it produces.

    • Entity
    • Description
    • Key attributes
    • Related entity
    • Relationship (for example, places)
    • Cardinality (one to one, one to many or many to many)
    • Optional or mandatory
    • Source system
    • Data dictionary link

    Sector supplements for data cataloguing

    The method on this page works for any critical data asset. Some kinds of data need more: extra rules, fields or safeguards. Those live in one place, the Sectors section, and are listed here with what each adds to data cataloguing.

    Sector supplements that add to data cataloguing
    SupplementWhat it coversWhat it adds to cataloguing
    Health and care dataFor critical data assets that hold patient-level or care records. Adds the health roles, duties and coding standards the core playbooks do not cover.4 extra catalogue fields and 2 identifiers
    Adult and children's social care dataFor assets built from council returns, such as adult social care client level data and children's social care census returns, where the source systems sit outside the policy's scope.3 extra catalogue fields and 1 identifiers
    Geospatial and address dataFor assets that hold addresses, coordinates or map geometry. Adds geospatial metadata, open identifiers and positional quality.4 extra catalogue fields and 3 identifiers
    Official statistics and administrative sourcesFor assets used to produce official statistics. Joins the DQAP to quality assurance of administrative data, so quality is reported once for both purposes.2 extra catalogue fields and 1 identifiers
    AI-ready dataFor assets used, or likely to be used, for AI or automated decision support. Turns the GOV.UK AI-ready data guidance into checks at the annual review.4 extra catalogue fields
    Justice, policing and national securityFor assets in law enforcement, justice, defence or security settings, where only OFFICIAL assets are in scope and metadata itself may be sensitive.2 extra catalogue fields
    Data about peopleFor any asset that holds records about individual people. Makes sure forms and rules let people describe themselves accurately.2 extra catalogue fields
    Reference data and identifiersFor assets that depend on code lists and identifiers maintained by other organisations, such as area codes, organisation codes and company numbers.1 extra catalogue fields and 2 identifiers
