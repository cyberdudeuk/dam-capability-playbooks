    Capability model

    Everything the DAM Policy and its related guidance need an organisation to be able to do, how each need is stated, and how mature you are.

    • 32 capabilities
    • 84 signposts
    • 10 topics, 6 themes, 5 levels

    In brief

    • 15 capabilities are explicit in the policy, 11 implicit and 6 inferred.
    • Every flag points to the section of GOV.UK guidance it comes from.
    • Assess yourself against the government maturity model and four DAM strands.

    The DAM Policy names three core capabilities, but meeting it depends on more. This model sets out 32 capabilities the policy, its guidance and adjacent rulebooks need, grouped in six clusters. Each is flagged by how the DAM Policy itself treats it.

    The six themes

    • Uses: Getting value from data: decisions, evidence of impact, better services.
    • Data: The technical side of managing data: assets, collection, quality, interoperability.
    • Leadership: How senior and business leaders engage with data: strategy, responsibility, oversight, investment.
    • Culture: Attitudes to data across the organisation: awareness, openness, security.
    • Tools: The systems and tools used to store, share and work with data.
    • Skills: The data skills and knowledge of staff at every level.

    Ownership and governanceFew assets have a named owner; decisions about data are made by whoever holds it.Owners named for some critical assets, often by job title only; no regular review.Every reported critical asset has a named owner who knows the role; a board meets and records decisions.Owners review their assets each year, escalate risks and are covered when people move; ownership is in objectives.Ownership is routine across the organisation and its bodies; owners shape investment and sharing decisions.
