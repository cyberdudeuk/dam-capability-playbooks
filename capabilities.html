    Capability playbooks

    Activities and templates for the capabilities beyond governance, cataloguing and quality that the DAM Policy depends on: strategy, tools, risk, suppliers, skills, culture, maturity, investment, assurance, legacy, handover, benefits and exit.

    • 13 capabilities
    • 40 activities
    • 16 templates
    • As of 2026-11-17

    In brief

    • 13 capabilities, 40 activities and 16 templates.
    • Each capability has activities for before your first return, before your second, and for business as usual.
    • Each links to the same capability in the capability model, the knowledge areas, the data portals compass and the asset governance explorer.

    Draft. Draft by this portal for review by the professions that own each capability. Not government guidance.

    The DAM Policy names three capabilities, but meeting it rests on more: strategy, tools, risk, suppliers, skills, culture, maturity, investment, assurance, legacy, handover, benefits and exit. Each capability below has activities for before your first return, before your second, and for business as usual, with a template to start from.

    Names and email addresses. Your own working copies can name the people who hold each role and give their work email addresses, handled under your organisation's data protection rules. Do not publish them: anything you put on GitHub or anywhere public should name roles only.

    New to this, or leading it? Start with what to aim for in your first year, then come back to the capabilities you need.

    Three stages

    1. Start: before your first returnWhat the DAM Policy needs now, so the return is complete and true.
    2. Build: before your second returnMakes the next return easier, and makes critical assets safer to reuse.
    3. Embed: business as usualKeeps the work going without a project, and prepares critical assets for AI and wider sharing.

    Data strategy and prioritisation by reuse value

    Business as usual and change

    See this capability in: Capability model · Capability playbook · Knowledge area activities (1) · Data portals compass · Asset governance explorer

    Why it matters for the DAM Policy: The policy asks you to identify critical data assets, but not which to improve first. A short, published way of ranking them by reuse value, outcomes and risk turns the list into a plan and shows why some assets come first.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

    Usually led by: Chief data officer. Content owner: The chief data officer, with strategy and portfolio teams.

    • STR-1 · Start: before your first return

      Rank critical assets by what they make possible

      Goal: You can say which critical assets matter most and why, in one page.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Steps
      1. List the critical assets you are reporting.
      2. Score each on reuse value, the outcomes it supports, the people affected and the risk if it fails, using the scorecard.
      3. Note where legacy systems, poor quality or missing ownership hold an asset back.
      4. Agree the top ten with the data governance board and record the decision.

    Data risk management

    Business as usual and change

    See this capability in: Capability model · Capability playbook · Knowledge area activities (3) · Data portals compass · Asset governance explorer

    Why it matters for the DAM Policy: A critical data asset that is lost, wrong or misused is a risk to services and people. Recording those risks on the same registers as other risks puts them in front of the people who decide.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

    Usually led by: Data owner. Content owner: Data owners, with the risk and assurance teams.

    Activities done0 of 3 done

    Already in the knowledge areas

    • MOD-4 Control changes to models
    • SEC-3 Link critical assets to cyber risk
    • BI-3 Make analytical copies traceable to their source

    Delivery and change actions this takes further

    • CH-07 Manage data asset risk on the same registers

    • RISK-1 · Start: before your first return

      Record the main risk to each critical asset

      Goal: Every critical asset has at least one risk on a register someone reviews.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Steps
      1. For each critical asset, ask what would happen if it was unavailable, wrong, exposed or not kept.
      2. Record the worst credible risk on the corporate or programme risk register, with the owner as risk owner.
      3. Score it with your organisation's risk method, and link it to the asset's catalogue record.
      You produce
      • Data risks on the register
      Evidence it is done
      Each critical asset has a linked risk with an owner.
      Measure
      Critical assets with a recorded risk.
      Who does what
      • Accountable: Data owner
      • Responsible (does the work): Data steward
      • Consulted: Lead policy coordinator
      • Informed: Data governance board

      Set risk appetite for data

      Goal: Owners know how much risk they can accept and when to escalate.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Steps
      1. Agree a risk appetite statement for data with the board, for availability, accuracy, privacy and retention.
      2. Set escalation thresholds and who they go to.
      3. Use exceptions, with an expiry date, for risks accepted outside appetite.
      You produce
      • Data risk appetite statement
      Evidence it is done
      The board has approved a data risk appetite.
      Measure
      Risks outside appetite with an action or a dated exception.

    Data skills, capability and literacy

    Business as usual

    See this capability in: Capability model · Capability playbook · Data portals compass · Asset governance explorer

    Why it matters for the DAM Policy: Owners, stewards and catalogue managers are often new to the role and doing it part time. Knowing what each role needs to know, and giving them a short route to it, is what makes the other capabilities work.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

    Usually led by: Chief data officer. Content owner: The chief data officer with learning and development teams.

    Activities done0 of 3 done

    Delivery and change actions this takes further

    • CH-09 Plan people change, not just process change

    See this capability in: Capability model · Capability playbook · Knowledge area activities (2) · Data portals compass · Asset governance explorer

    Why it matters for the DAM Policy: Today's new system is tomorrow's legacy. Requiring open formats, documented schemas and a tested exit at the start keeps critical data movable for as long as it is needed.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

    Usually led by: Design authority or solution architect. Content owner: Design authorities and commercial teams.

    • EXIT-1 · Start: before your first return

      Ask three questions of every new system

      Goal: No new system holds critical data you cannot get out.O: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance

      Steps
      1. Ask whether the data can be exported in full, in an open format, with a published schema.
      2. Ask whether the export has been tested.
      3. Ask what leaving would cost and how long it would take.
