    Routes by role Level: Foundation

    Start from your role

    Choose the role closest to yours. Each route shows what you need to learn, what to do and in what order, and tracks your progress as you go.

    In brief

    • Pick the role closest to yours. Roles describe responsibilities, not job titles; you may hold more than one.
    • Each route has five stages: learn, prepare, do, report and improve.
    • Mark steps as done to track your progress. It is saved in this browser only.
    Easy read summary
    • Choose the job closest to yours.
    • You will see the steps for your job in order.
    • Tick each step when you finish it.

    Chief data officer

    You are accountable for adopting the policy across your organisation and, in a department, its arm's length bodies.

    Your route0 of 9 done

    Your context

    You have little time, decide on resourcing and report progress to the Chief Data Officer Council. You need summaries first and detail on request.

    Your goals

    • Adopt the policy once, without the three capabilities duplicating effort
    • Know your organisation's position at any time
    • Show credible progress to the CDO Council

    What you need

    • As a chief data officer, I need see what the policy requires and who does what, so that I can assign the work with confidence.
    • As a chief data officer, I need a short, current view of progress, so that I can unblock problems before the return is due.

    What gets in the way

    • Governance, cataloguing and quality run by different teams record the same facts in different places
    • Evidence is assembled in a rush at the deadline

    We design for

    • Reads on a phone between meetings
    • Wants the summary before the detail

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    Every critical data asset has a named owner, a current catalogue record and a DQAP, and the return goes in on time.

    Lead policy coordinator

    You coordinate adoption day to day, collate and assure the return, and represent your organisation at the Working Group.

    Your route0 of 10 done

    Your context

    You work across many teams, often alongside another role. You need one route from start to return and templates you can reuse.

    Your goals

    • Get the baseline return right first time
    • Avoid retyping information that already exists
    • Keep arm's length bodies in step

    What you need

    • As a lead policy coordinator, I need one ordered route from mobilising to an assured return, so that nothing is missed and nothing is done twice.
    • As a lead policy coordinator, I need templates that match what GDS asks for, so that the return is collation, not reconstruction.

    What gets in the way

    • Requests arrive from several directions with different deadlines
    • Owners change and nobody updates the record

    We design for

    • Works compressed or part-time hours and picks tasks up after gaps
    • New to data management

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    An assured return goes in on time, built from catalogue records rather than retyped.

    Data owner

    You are the senior leader accountable for the meaning, content, quality and management of one or more critical data assets.

    Your route0 of 8 done

    Your context

    Data ownership is one of several responsibilities. You make decisions and sign off; others do the day-to-day work.

    Your goals

    • Understand exactly what you are accountable for
    • Make decisions quickly with the right evidence
    • Name a successor so the asset is never orphaned

    What you need

    • As a data owner, I need a clear list of the decisions that are mine, so that I can sign off with confidence.
    • As a data owner, I need to see how good my data is and what it should not be used for, so that I can accept or reject risks knowingly.

    What gets in the way

    • Unclear difference between data owner and information asset owner
    • Asked to sign things without the context

    We design for

    • Uses screen magnification at 200 per cent
    • Prefers checklists to long text

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    You can explain your assets' purpose, quality and risks, and your successor is named.

    Data steward

    You run critical data assets day to day - their standards, access, metadata and quality processes.

    Your route0 of 10 done

    Your context

    You do the detailed work and keep the records current. You need step-by-step methods and templates.

    Your goals

    • Keep the catalogue record and the DQAP current without heroics
    • Find problems before users do
    • Hand over cleanly when you move on

    What you need

    • As a data steward, I need step-by-step instructions with templates, so that I can do the work consistently across assets.
    • As a data steward, I need one place to record each fact once, so that the catalogue, the DQAP and the register always agree.

    What gets in the way

    • The same owner and review date recorded in three places
    • Quality rules kept only in one tool

    We design for

    • Dyslexic and prefers short steps
    • Uses a screen reader for long documents

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    Every asset you steward has a current record, rules with targets, and a quality statement users understand.

    Catalogue manager or data custodian

    You run the catalogue and the systems behind it, so records can be found, harvested and kept accurate.

    Your route0 of 7 done

    Your context

    You work with tools and standards. You need to know what any catalogue must expose, whatever the product.

    Your goals

    • Records that machines can harvest, not only people
    • Conform to the cross-government metadata standard
    • Avoid lock-in to one product

    What you need

    • As a catalogue manager or data custodian, I need the exact metadata properties and how they map to the standard, so that records conform first time.
    • As a catalogue manager or data custodian, I need to know what the Discoverability Platform will expect, so that we do not rebuild the feed later.

    What gets in the way

    • Guidance and model do not list the same properties
    • Internal-only links break outside the organisation

    We design for

    • Works mainly by keyboard
    • English is an additional language

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    Every critical data asset record is complete, conforms to the standard and can be harvested as DCAT.

    Data quality lead or analyst

    You bring methods and tools for measuring and improving quality across many assets.

    Your route0 of 8 done

    Your context

    You support several stewards. You need a consistent scoring method and tools that work without installing anything.

    Your goals

    • Score issues the same way across assets
    • Automate measurement across assets
    • Show quality fairly for every group the data describes

    What you need

    • As a data quality lead or analyst, I need the government scoring method and tools that apply it, so that priorities are consistent and defensible.
    • As a data quality lead or analyst, I need checks that break results down by group, so that poor quality for some people is not averaged away.

    What gets in the way

    • Every team scores issues differently
    • Quality reported as one average

    We design for

    • Colour-blind, so charts must not rely on colour alone
    • Works across several devices

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    Every critical data asset has scored issues, automated checks and quality reported by group where lawful.

    Programme or project lead

    You lead a medium or large change programme or project that creates, changes, migrates or retires critical data assets.

    Your route0 of 9 done

    Your context

    Your programme has its own governance, gates, budget and suppliers. Data asset management can feel like someone else's job until a gate review, a migration or handover exposes the gaps.

    Your goals

    • Pass assurance gates without late data surprises
    • Hand over assets with owners and quality plans in place
    • Avoid creating the next legacy system

    What you need

    • As a programme or project lead, I need to know which DAM duties apply to my programme and when, so that they are planned and funded from the business case.
    • As a programme or project lead, I need clear data roles inside my programme team, so that nobody assumes someone else is doing it.

    What gets in the way

    • Data owners sit outside the programme and are hard to reach
    • Migration quality problems surface only at cutover

    We design for

    • Reads on the move between meetings
    • Needs a one-page view to share with the programme board

    Illustrative access needs, not drawn from research with this audience (OR-04).

    Success looks like

    Every critical asset the programme touches has an owner, a catalogue entry and a quality plan before the programme closes.
