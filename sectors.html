    Supplements in full

    SUP-HLT · Must have

    Health and care data

    Governance Cataloguing Quality

    For critical data assets that hold patient-level or care records. Adds the health roles, duties and coding standards the core playbooks do not cover.

    For: NHS England and health bodies; Department of Health and Social Care ALBs. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Map the data owner and steward to the Caldicott Guardian, the SIRO and, for health IT, the clinical safety officer. Record who holds each role for every asset.
    • Record the legal basis for each use, the common law duty of confidentiality position and whether the national data opt-out applies.
    • Check the organisation's latest Data Security and Protection Toolkit assessment covers the systems that hold the asset.
    • Apply the Caldicott principles when deciding what each user of the asset needs: the minimum necessary, justified for each purpose.
    • Record the clinical terminology and exchange standards the asset follows, such as SNOMED CT, dm+d and HL7 FHIR UK Core, in conformsTo, so the MEM record points to the sector standard.
    • Where a health IT system holds the asset, record its clinical safety position: DCB0129 for the supplier and DCB0160 for the organisation that deploys it.
    • If confidential patient information is used without consent, record the legal basis that sets aside the duty of confidentiality, such as section 251 support, separately from the data protection lawful basis.
    • If software or AI that uses the asset has a medical purpose, check whether it is a medical device under the Medical Devices Regulations 2002, using the MHRA's guidance.
    • If NHS health and social care data in the asset is used for research or analysis beyond direct care, give access through a secure data environment, as DHSC's secure data environment policy guidelines require, and record which environment in the catalogue record.

    Risks to check

    • Pseudonymised data is still personal data; access controls and agreements still apply.
    • Opt-outs and local suppression can bias coverage for some groups; check coverage against an independent count.
    • Clinical codes can be valid in format but wrong in meaning; format checks do not replace clinical coding audit.

    SUP-SOC · Should have

    Adult and children's social care data

    Cataloguing Quality

    For assets built from council returns, such as adult social care client level data and children's social care census returns, where the source systems sit outside the policy's scope.

    For: Local authorities (voluntary adoption); Department of Health and Social Care; Department for Education. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Record which councils supply the data and that they are not bound by the DAM Policy; agree quality expectations through the collection's own guidance.
    • Name the owner for the national asset and a contact route for suppliers, not individuals.
    • Record changes to collection specifications as decisions, because they change what the asset means.

    Risks to check

    • Councils use different case management systems, so the same field can be captured differently.
    • Late or missing submissions make national figures look better or worse than they are.
    • Linking to health data depends on NHS number quality in council systems.

    SUP-GEO · Must have

    Geospatial and address data

    Cataloguing Quality

    For assets that hold addresses, coordinates or map geometry. Adds geospatial metadata, open identifiers and positional quality.

    For: Geospatial Commission; Ordnance Survey; HM Land Registry; environment bodies. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Record the licence for any third-party geospatial data the asset depends on, and who may use derived data.
    • Use the open identifiers for property and street (UPRN and USRN) as the join between assets.
    • Apply the Geospatial Commission's guidance on ethical use of location data when location could identify people.

    Risks to check

    • A valid UPRN format does not prove the property exists or is the right one; check match rates against the authoritative address base.
    • Coordinates in mixed reference systems silently misplace features.
    • Small-area location data can identify people even when names are removed.

    SUP-STA · Should have

    Official statistics and administrative sources

    Quality Cataloguing

    For assets used to produce official statistics. Joins the DQAP to quality assurance of administrative data, so quality is reported once for both purposes.

    For: Office for National Statistics; departmental statistics teams; Office for Statistics Regulation. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Record the statistics produced from the asset and the producer responsible for them.
    • Agree with the statistics producer how supplier changes are communicated before they happen.
    • Use the DQAP as evidence for the assurance level set under quality assurance of administrative data.

    Risks to check

    • Operational changes in the supplier's system can look like real-world change.
    • Two quality reports for the same data drift apart unless one feeds the other.

    SUP-SHR · Must have

    Data sharing and legal gateways

    Governance

    For every critical data asset that includes personal data, because critical data assets are defined by being shared. Adds a legal gateway record to the governance and catalogue records.

    For: All departments. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Keep a sharing decision record for each flow: purpose, lawful basis, any special category condition, legal power or gateway, DPIA reference, agreement reference and review date.
    • Check each flow against the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes to UK GDPR at the next review.
    • Use a data sharing agreement for routine sharing, as the ICO code recommends, even where it is not mandatory.
    • Name the owner on both sides of every flow, by role.
    • If the flow uses a Part 5 power in the Digital Economy Act 2017, check the agreement is on the published register.
    • Record the agreement with the data sharing agreement template (T-GOV-6), and name the legal gateway in it and in the catalogue record.

    Risks to check

    • Extra fields creep into extracts over time, beyond what the agreement allows.
    • Lawful basis recorded once at the start of a flow can go out of date when the law or the purpose changes.

    SUP-AI · Must have

    AI-ready data

    Quality Governance Cataloguing

    For assets used, or likely to be used, for AI or automated decision support. Turns the GOV.UK AI-ready data guidance into checks at the annual review.

    For: All departments using data for AI or automated decision support. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Record the AI task the data supports, whether outputs affect individuals, and which uses are out of scope, such as automated decisions.
    • Name the owner who approves AI uses, and decide what happens if a use changes.
    • Publish an algorithmic transparency record where a tool supports decisions about people.
    • Use the Data and AI Ethics Framework for new or higher-risk uses.

    Risks to check

    • Open or available data is not the same as AI-ready data.
    • Bias in labels or coverage becomes bias in outputs.
    • Without exact versioning, a model cannot be retrained or audited.

    SUP-REC · Should have

    Records, retention and disposal

    Governance

    For every critical data asset that must be kept, archived or destroyed on a schedule. Joins data asset management to records management.

    For: All departments; knowledge and information management teams. A synthesis of the sources belowO: This playbook's own synthesis of published guidance.

    Governance overlay

    • Record the retention schedule and disposal action for each asset in the ownership register.
    • Agree with the records manager when an asset is retired, archived or transferred.
    • Keep catalogue records for retired assets, marked as retired, so dependants can see what changed.
    • When an asset needs to be kept longer than its schedule allows, record the reason and who approved it in the exception register (T-GOV-4), with a review date.

    Risks to check

    • Data kept past its retention period is a data protection risk; data destroyed early is a records risk.
    • There is no single government-wide retention period. The National Archives describes short, medium and long periods and each organisation sets its own schedule, so check yours before using an example such as seven years.
