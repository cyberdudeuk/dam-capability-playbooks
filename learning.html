    AI-ready data stewardship

    Six modules for owners, stewards and catalogue managers who are asked whether a critical data asset can be used for AI. They build from what AI-ready means, through metadata and linked data, to governing AI use, and join this portal to five sister projects so you do not have to find them yourself.

    How it lines up with Data Stewardship at a Time of AI, a deep-dive series

    Data Stewards Academy (The Data Tank and The GovLab). Five weekly live sessions of 90 minutes, Wednesdays 10 March to 7 April 2027, as published on 14 November 2026. An independent course, not connected with this portal. The rows below show where this pathway prepares you for, or follows up, each session. Check dates and details on the course page. Course page.

    What is still missing

    • PG-01 Participatory and community-led stewardship Open
      No project in this estate yet gives guidance on data cooperatives, community charters or involving the public in decisions about AI use of their data. M16 points to the inclusion questions only.
    • PG-02 Questions-led demand for data Started
      M14 asks you to write down the question an AI use serves. There is no method yet for gathering and prioritising questions from users and the public.
    • PG-03 International comparison Open
      The pathway is UK-focused. It does not yet compare how other governments prepare public data for AI.

    The case against

    Another learning route spreads people thin. Stewards could go straight to the GDS guidelines and an external course, and a pathway that links to five prototypes inherits their churn and their unendorsed status.

    Our answer

    The pathway adds no new guidance of its own. It reuses the GDS guidelines, the existing AI-ready supplement and sister projects, labels each one's status, and keeps the checks of understanding to facts you can trace. If a sister project moves or closes, the weekly health run reports the broken link.

    Learn it, keep it, share it

    Learning sticks when you use it, come back to it and explain it to someone else. These tips help you learn from the playbooks and the sources they cite, keep what you learn, share it, and count it towards your role, your career and continuing professional development (CPD).

    Learn it

    • Start from a real asset you look after. Read each module with that asset in mind, not in the abstract.
    • Do the activities, not only the reading. Each one produces something you need for the return anyway.
    • Read the source behind a point when it matters: every fact links to the GOV.UK page or standard it comes from.
    • Use the glossary and tooltips the first time a term is new. Hover or focus on a dotted term to see what it means.
    • Follow the journey for your role first, then pick other modules as your work needs them.

    Keep it

    • Try the check of understanding before you reread the module. Recalling an answer helps you keep it more than reading again.
    • Come back to a module a week later and a month later. The page reminds you when a module is due for a refresher.
    • Write one sentence after each module: what you will do differently on Monday.
    • Keep your filled templates. They are a record of what you did and a reminder next year.
    • Use your progress record as a checklist before each annual return.

    Share it

    • Explain one module to a colleague in ten minutes. Teaching it is the quickest way to find what you have not understood.
    • Run a lunch-and-learn on a playbook with your owners and stewards, using the module activities as the agenda.
    • Post a question or a lesson in the cross-government data management community, and in the playbooks' discussions.
    • Suggest a fix when something here is unclear: every page has a link to report a problem or suggest a change.
    • Share your filled templates inside your organisation so the next person starts from yours.

    Your role, career and certifications

    What you learn here counts towards your role, your profession and your next job. The modules map to roles in the Digital and Data Capability Framework and to government professions, so you can use them as evidence in a development conversation, a job application or a promotion.

    1. Find your role, or the one you want, on the Professions and skills page, and note its skills.
    2. Pick the modules that build those skills, and add them to your development plan.
    3. Record each module you finish, what you did with it and the evidence, in your CPD log.
    4. Use the record in your performance review and in Success Profiles applications.
    5. When you are ready, choose a certification that fits your role and aim for it.

    Modules in full

    M01 · Foundation · 15 minutes

    The DAM Policy - what it asks of you

    Governance Cataloguing Quality
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Explain who the policy applies to and what is in scope
    • Name the core duties: identify and catalogue assets, report them, name an owner and manage quality
    • Find the requirement behind any step in these playbooks

    Activities

    1. Read. Read the summary of who the policy applies to and what is in scope.
    2. Read. Scan the requirements table. Filter it to 'must' to see the core duties.
    3. Do. Note which of the reporting dates applies to your organisation first.
    4. Reflect. Which of the three capabilities is least developed in your organisation today?

    Check your understanding

    Which organisations must follow the policy?

    Show the answer

    UK government departments and their arm's length bodies. It applies to UK government departments and their ALBs. It is optional for public corporations and does not apply to councils.

    What must every identified data asset have?

    Show the answer

    A named data owner and a DQAP or equivalent. The policy requires a named data owner and a data quality action plan, or an equivalent mechanism, for every identified asset.

    M02 · Foundation · 15 minutes

    Roles and accountability

    Governance
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Describe the data owner, data steward and data custodian roles
    • Explain how the data owner relates to the information asset owner
    • Choose between the three ownership options

    Activities

    1. Read. Read the role model shared by all three playbooks.
    2. Read. Read the Assign recipe and its checklist.
    3. Do. List your critical data assets and the role that should own each one.
    4. Reflect. Does every owner in your list have a named successor?

    Check your understanding

    Which role is accountable for an asset's meaning, content and quality?

    Show the answer

    Data owner. The data owner is the senior leader accountable. The steward runs the asset day to day; the custodian runs the technical system.

    What must happen when a data owner leaves?

    Show the answer

    A successor is ready and the metadata is updated. The guidance asks you to tell the coordinating team, have a successor ready and update the metadata.

    M03 · Practitioner · 30 minutes

    Setting up governance with five recipes

    Governance
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Run the Mandate, Frame, Assign, Operate and Assure recipes in order
    • Set up a decision log and an exception register
    • Collect evidence continuously for the GDS return

    Activities

    1. Read. Work through the five recipes step by step.
    2. Do. Draft a one-page charter using the template.
    3. Do. Set up a decision log and an exception register using the templates.
    4. Reflect. Where will your evidence folder live, and who keeps it current?

    Check your understanding

    What makes an exception different from a policy change?

    Show the answer

    It has an owner and an expiry date. An exception is time-bound with an owner and an expiry date. Without an expiry it is an undocumented policy change.

    When should you assemble evidence for the return?

    Show the answer

    Continuously during Operate. Collecting evidence each time a decision is logged turns the return into collation rather than reconstruction.

    M04 · Practitioner · 25 minutes

    Finding and designating critical data assets

    Cataloguing
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Build a candidate list from existing registers
    • Apply the scope tests and the seven purposes
    • Draw an asset's boundary and record why

    Activities

    1. Read. Read stages 1 to 3 of the cataloguing method.
    2. Do. Start a candidate log using the designation template.
    3. Do. For one candidate, record which of the seven purposes it supports.
    4. Reflect. Have you looked at data you acquire from others, not only data you provide?

    Check your understanding

    If you designate a distribution of a data set, what else should you designate?

    Show the answer

    Its source data set. The guidance asks you to designate the source data set as well.

    Which of these is one of the seven purposes?

    Show the answer

    Official statistics. Official statistics is one of the seven. The statistics themselves are not designated, only the data used to produce them.

    M05 · Practitioner · 30 minutes

    Describing assets with metadata others can use

    Cataloguing
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Record the metadata properties in the GOV.UK guidance
    • Write titles and descriptions in plain English
    • Map properties to the Metadata Exchange Model

    Activities

    1. Read. Read the metadata properties and how to record each one.
    2. Do. Complete a catalogue record for one asset using the template.
    3. Do. Ask someone outside your team to read the record and say what the data is for.

    M06 · Practitioner · 20 minutes

    Cataloguing, harvesting and reporting to GDS

    Cataloguing
    Activities done0 of 3 done

    • Expose records so others can harvest them
    • Produce the return from catalogue records
    • Plan the annual review

    2. Do. Check that every link in one record works from outside your network.
    3. Do. Put the annual review date in the same diary entry as the DQAP review.

    Show the answer

    M07 · Foundation · 15 minutes

    Data quality foundations

    Quality
    Activities done0 of 3 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Name the five principles and six dimensions of the Government Data Quality Framework
    • Explain what a DQAP is for
    • Recognise when quality differs between groups of people

    Activities

    1. Read. Read the principles and dimensions.
    2. Read. Read the overview of the quality playbook.
    3. Reflect. Which dimension matters most for your most important decision?

    Check your understanding

    Which is one of the six dimensions?

    Show the answer

    Timeliness. The six are completeness, uniqueness, consistency, timeliness, validity and accuracy.

    Why break quality results down by group?

    Show the answer

    So poor quality for some people is not averaged away. An average can hide poor quality for some groups, which can exclude them from services.

    M08 · Practitioner · 35 minutes

    Building a DQAP and scoring issues

    Quality
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Write rules with targets from the issues framework
    • Measure a baseline and log issues
    • Prioritise issues and set resolution dates

    Activities

    1. Read. Work through the eight quality stages step by step.
    2. Do. Write three rules for one critical data element using the template.
    3. Do. Score an issue with the issues framework calculator. (Public Sector Data Quality Commons)
    4. Reflect. Which issue would you fix at source rather than clean downstream?

    Check your understanding

    How does the issues framework set a rule's target range?

    Show the answer

    From importance of purpose times impact. Importance of purpose multiplied by impact gives a combined score, which sets the target range.

    In the framework's example timescales, when is a critical issue resolved?

    Show the answer

    Immediately. The framework's example gives Critical as immediate; High, Medium and Low within 1, 3 and 6 measurements.

    M12 · Foundation · 20 minutes

    What makes a critical data asset AI-ready

    Governance Cataloguing Quality AI-ready data stewardship
    Activities done0 of 4 done

    By the end you will be able to

    • Name the four pillars in the GDS guidelines on AI-ready datasets
    • Decide whether an asset needs the AI-ready supplement
    • Explain why AI-readiness is a stewardship question as well as a technical one

    Activities

    1. Read. Read the executive summary and the four pillars of the GDS guidelines on making government datasets ready for AI. (Making government datasets ready for AI)
    2. Do. Answer the 'which supplements apply' questions for one asset. Note whether it is used, or may be used, for AI.
    3. Read. Read the AI-ready data supplement: what governance, cataloguing and quality each add.
    4. Reflect. If someone asked tomorrow to train a model on your most important asset, who would say yes or no, and on what evidence?

    Check your understanding

    Which of these is one of the four pillars of AI-ready data in the GDS guidelines?

    Show the answer

    Data and metadata quality. The four pillars are technical optimisation; data and metadata quality; organisation and infrastructure context; and legal, security and ethical compliance.

    When does the AI-ready supplement ask you to publish an algorithmic transparency record?

    Show the answer

    Where a tool supports decisions about people. The supplement asks for a record where an algorithmic tool supports decisions about people, using the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard.

    M13 · Practitioner · 30 minutes

    From FAIR to AI-ready metadata

    Cataloguing AI-ready data stewardship
    Activities done0 of 5 done
