    Data governance

    Who decides, by what authority, and how do we know it is working?

    • 5 steps
    • 8 roles
    • 4 templates
    • 6 learning modules

    In brief

    • Every critical data asset needs a named data owner. Governance is how you appoint them and keep them active.
    • Run five recipes in order the first time, then as a yearly loop.
    • Record decisions, issues and exceptions as you go, so your evidence for GDS builds itself.
    Easy read summary
    • Data governance means deciding who looks after data and how.
    • This page shows the steps to set that up.
    • It has templates you can copy.

    Why it matters

    Put the authority, roles and routines in place so that every critical data asset has a named, active owner, decisions are made and recorded, and the organisation can evidence its DAM Policy adoption.

    The policy requires a named data owner for every asset (DAM-R05), an ownership option that still meets the mandatory IAO actions (DAM-R09), and CDO-led implementation under CDO Council oversight (DAM-R08).

    Before you start

    • Agreement from your chief data officer to lead adoption
    • A list of the data policies you already have, however informal
    • Your organisation's choice, or shortlist, of ownership option

    What you will have at the end

    • A one-page charter
    • An ownership register
    • Live decision and exception logs
    • An evidence pack for the GDS return

    Five recipesP: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published

    Five repeatable recipes: Mandate, Frame, Assign, Operate and Assure. Run them in order the first time, then as a loop, with Assure feeding lessons back into Mandate. The sequence comes from the cross-government Data Governance Playbook (practitioner draft); the requirements it meets come from GDS guidance.

    Your progress through data governance0 of 5 done

    1. 1

      Step 1: Mandate

      Not started

      Establish the authority everything else operates under.

      When: A new governance need, the start of DAM Policy adoption, or the annual refresh.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Confirm the sponsor (the CDO, SIRO or equivalent) who will chair or co-sign the charter.
      2. Draft a one-page charter covering purpose, scope, authority and review cadence.
      3. State which data, systems and decisions are in scope, and any exclusions. Start narrow, with critical data assets.
      4. Route the charter for approval and record the decision and its date.
      5. Publish it to every stakeholder found during scoping.

      You will have

      • Governance charter
      • Board terms of reference
      • Scope statement with exclusions
      • Sign-off record

      Check before you move on

      • Sponsor confirmed in writing
      • Scope covers every critical data asset the organisation owns
      • Sign-off obtained and dated
      • Review date set, at least annual

      Meets: DAM-R08 DAM-R14

      Tip. A charter that tries to cover everything is approved slowly or not at all. Widen scope at the annual refresh once the model works.

    2. 2

      Step 2: Frame

      Not started

      Assemble a proportionate rulebook to make decisions against.

      When: The charter is approved and policy gaps are known.Accountable: Chief data officer, Data governance board

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. List every data policy and standard already in force, however informal.
      2. Compare it with the core areas - governance, classification and handling, quality, retention and disposal, access and sharing, metadata, master and reference data, and ethics including AI use.
      3. Draft or adopt what is missing, in proportion to the risk. Reuse cross-government guidance before writing your own.
      4. Give every policy a named owner (a role) and a review date.
      5. Register each policy and make it findable, ideally in the data catalogue.

      You will have

      • Policy and standards register
      • Gap assessment
      • Policy owner list with review dates

      Check before you move on

      • Gap assessment against the core areas done
      • Every policy has an owner role and a review date
      • The register is published and findable

      Meets: DAM-R09

      Tip. Adopting a cross-government template keeps you consistent with other departments and makes shared assurance easier.

    3. 3

      Step 3: Assign

      Not started

      Put a named, accountable owner and an active steward against every critical data asset.

      When: Policies are adopted and critical assets lack named owners.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Choose your ownership option - IAO model, data ownership model or hybrid - and record the decision.
      2. For each critical data asset, propose an owner of matching seniority (SCS for large or complex assets; Grade 6 or 7 for smaller ones), a steward and a custodian.
      3. Agree decision rights using the RACI below and any delegated thresholds.
      4. Brief role holders and record training (Data Owner and Steward training).
      5. Record owner, steward, custodian, effective date and a named successor in the ownership register and the catalogue record.

      You will have

      • Ownership option decision
      • Data ownership register
      • RACI per asset or asset class
      • Succession plan

      Check before you move on

      • Every critical data asset has a named owner
      • Every role has a named deputy or successor
      • The catalogue record shows the owner role and a contact point that is a team or form, never a personal inbox
      • Mandatory IAO actions still covered whichever option you chose

      Meets: DAM-R05 DAM-R09 DAM-R10

      Tip. A single point of failure in accountability is the most common finding when this step is audited. Name a backup for every owner and steward.

    4. 4

      Step 4: Operate

      Not started

      Make governance visible day to day.

      When: Owners are in place and governance moves into business as usual.Accountable: Data governance board

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Set the board's cadence (monthly, at least quarterly) and publish the calendar.
      2. Keep a decision log - what, who, why, when and the review trigger.
      3. Keep an issue and risk log, with quality issues coming in from the DQAP.
      4. Log every deviation from policy as an exception with an owner and an expiry date.
      5. Escalate anything unresolved up the ladder.

      You will have

      • Decision log
      • Issue and risk log
      • Exception register
      • Board minutes

      Check before you move on

      • Decision log active and current
      • Every exception has an expiry date
      • Escalations tracked to resolution
      • Minutes published within five working days

      Meets: DAM-R05

      Tip. An empty decision log after three months is a warning, not efficiency. Decisions are being made off the record.

    5. 5

      Step 5: Assure

      Not started

      Prove it works and feed the lessons back into the mandate.

      When: A reporting cycle, the annual return or an audit is due.Accountable: Chief data officer

      How to do it

      Do this

      1. Re-run the data maturity self-assessment.
      2. Compile measures from the live registers - owner coverage, review currency, DQAP coverage, exceptions.
      3. Map evidence to the GDS annual return as you go, not at the deadline.
      4. Have someone outside the board check the evidence before submission.
      5. Submit, then log lessons back into Mandate.

      You will have

      • Maturity score and narrative
      • Measures pack
      • Evidence pack mapped to the GDS return
      • Lessons note

      Check before you move on

      • Evidence collected continuously during Operate
      • Independent check done before submission
      • Return submitted on time
      • Lessons logged to Mandate

      Meets: DAM-R03 DAM-R08

      Tip. Keep an evidence folder updated every time a decision is logged, so the return is collation, not reconstruction.

    Roles

    • Chief data officer (CDO)V: Verified against the primary source
      Implements the policy in the organisation and leads the identification exercise; sponsors governance.
    • Data governance board (BRD)P: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published
      Sets policy direction, resolves escalations and sees every asset's status. Chaired by the CDO, SIRO or equivalent.
    • Lead policy coordinator (COO)P: Practitioner material from cross-government playbooks not yet published
      Coordinates identification, collates and assures the return (including ALBs), and is the link to the Working Group.
    • Data owner (OWN)V: Verified against the primary source
      Senior leader accountable for the meaning, content, quality and management of the asset; approves changes and signs off targets and risks.
    • Information asset owner (IAO)V: Verified against the primary source
      Accountable to the SIRO for protecting the whole information asset; may hold data owner duties under the IAO or hybrid option.
    • Data steward (STW)V: Verified against the primary source
      Runs the asset day to day: policies and standards, access, quality processes and documentation such as the business glossary in the catalogue.
    • Data custodian (CUS)V: Verified against the primary source
      Technical expert for the system holding the data: storage, access controls, auditable change and disposal.
    • Data protection officer (DPO)K: General knowledge, not checked against a source in this release
      Independent advice on lawful basis, DPIAs and data subject rights.

    Sector supplements for data governance

    The method on this page works for any critical data asset. Some kinds of data need more: extra rules, fields or safeguards. Those live in one place, the Sectors section, and are listed here with what each adds to data governance.

    Health and care dataFor critical data assets that hold patient-level or care records. Adds the health roles, duties and coding standards the core playbooks do not cover.4 governance points and 3 risks
    Data sharing and legal gatewaysFor every critical data asset that includes personal data, because critical data assets are defined by being shared. Adds a legal gateway record to the governance and catalogue records.4 governance points and 2 risks
    AI-ready dataFor assets used, or likely to be used, for AI or automated decision support. Turns the GOV.UK AI-ready data guidance into checks at the annual review.4 governance points and 3 risks

    Reference data and identifiersFor assets that depend on code lists and identifiers maintained by other organisations, such as area codes, organisation codes and company numbers.3 governance points and 1 risks
